Lesson 08 · Hold and pulse

The left hand holds, the right hand pulses

Four bars, four chords, and the first time your two hands do different things over the same stretch of time.

The pattern

Every bar works the same way. The left hand plays an octave once, on beat one, and holds it. Over that, the right hand plays a chord on every beat: one, two, three, four. Then both hands move to the next bar.

1C octaveC · E · GC
2F octaveC · F · AF
3G octaveD · G · BG
4A octaveC · E · AAm

Those are the same four chords as Lesson 6, in the order I–IV–V–vi. What's new is the texture: the left hand gives you the low end, and the right hand gives you the beat.

Left hand: octaves, pinky and thumb

An octave is the same letter eight white keys apart. Put your pinky on the low one and your thumb on the high one, and keep that stretch fixed. Then the whole hand moves as one shape: C to F, F to G, G to A. Look at the low note, and the thumb is already in place.

The octaves here sit below middle C: C2–C3, F2–F3, G2–G3 and A2–A3. On the staff, the pinky's note is the lower of the two, down on the ledger lines.

Right hand: four chords that hardly move

You could play these as root-position triads, with C–E–G, F–A–C, G–B–D and A–C–E all stacked from the bottom. But then your right hand would leap up the keyboard every bar. Instead, each chord is rearranged (an inversion) so it stays close to the one before:

Watch the thumb C → F: the thumb stays on C. The other two fingers each step up one key (E→F, G→A).
F → G: the whole shape shifts up one white key (C·F·A → D·G·B).
G → Am: back down, with the thumb returning to C.

For three bars out of four, your thumb sits on middle C. The left hand already plays each chord's name in the bass, so the right hand doesn't need the root at the bottom. That's what an inversion buys you.

Hands together: hold under the pulse

Here's the real skill. On beat one, both hands land together. Then the right hand lifts and strikes three more times, and the left hand stays down. Your hands will want to copy each other. Every time the right hand bounces, the left will want to bounce too. Don't let it.

On the staff, the line after each left-hand octave shows how long it has to be held. It turns blue while you're holding, green if you held through the fourth chord, and red if you let go early.

Now with the click

Same four bars, played twice through, against a metronome. Four clicks count you in. Then the left hand and the first chord land on the accented click, and the right hand plays one chord per click after that. Each note is graded in milliseconds against the beat, like Lesson 4. The left hand must still be down when the fourth click of its bar sounds. You can lift it right after that to move.

Check yourself

Between now and next time

Ten minutes. Two on the left-hand octaves until the hand jumps as a single shape. Three on the right-hand chords, saying which fingers move at each change. Five on the click drill. Start at 60, and go up only once holds kept reads four out of four and In time is above 80%.

Then switch the drill off and just play it, round and round. Once that feels easy, try the right hand in rhythm: one, two-and, three, four. That's the same pattern, and it sounds like a song.
