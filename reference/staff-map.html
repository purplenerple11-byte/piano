The staff map

Five anchors, two stories, one mirror. Print it and keep it on the music stand.

Story 1 · The three Cs are a ladder

CWhere it sitsDistance from middle C
C5Treble staff, 3rd spaceAn octave above
C4 (middle)Its own short line, between the staves—
C3Bass staff, 2nd spaceAn octave below

Three Cs, evenly spaced, one per staff plus the one in the middle. Middle C is the only note with its own private line — that short stroke belongs to no staff, which is exactly why it's easy to spot.

Story 2 · Each clef names its own note

ClefAlso calledWhat it marks
Treble 𝄞The G clefIts curl wraps the line that is G4
Bass 𝄢The F clefIts two dots straddle the line that is F3

The clef is not decoration and it is not a label for the staff — it is an arrow pointing at one specific note. Two anchors, free, drawn on every page of music you will ever open.

The mirror

The grand staff is symmetric around middle C. Every anchor above has a partner the same number of staff positions below:

Above middle CStepsBelow middle C
Treble G — clef curl4 ↔ 4Bass F — clef dots
C5 — treble 3rd space7 ↔ 7C3 — bass 2nd space
E4 — treble bottom line2 ↔ 2A3 — bass top line
F5 — treble top line10 ↔ 10G2 — bass bottom line
Why this is worth knowing Learn the top half and the bottom half comes free. Anything you can find above middle C, you can find the same distance below it by looking at the mirrored position. It also explains why the two clefs feel different: they are the same picture, reflected — not two unrelated alphabets to memorise.

Shapes, not letters

Once you're on an anchor, the page only moves two ways, and each one has a look:

MoveLooks likeOn the keys
StepLine → space, or space → line. A zigzag.Next white key. Neighbouring finger.
SkipLine → line, or space → space. A stack.Miss one white key. Finger gap.
The snowman A triad in root position is three notes all on lines, or all on spaces — three circles stacked straight up. It looks like a snowman, and once you see snowmen you stop reading chords note by note. Cross-reference: Lesson 6 built these under your hands; this is what they look like on the page.
